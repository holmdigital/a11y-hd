## TL;DR

`@holmdigital/standards@2.5.0` adds `us-hhs-section-504` — the HHS Digital Accessibility Final Rule — to the national laws database. It covers private organizations that receive federal financial assistance from the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS): hospitals, federally qualified health centers (FQHCs), long-term care facilities, health plans, and research institutions. The release shipped **six days before the hard compliance deadline of 2026-05-11** for entities with 15 or more employees.

- **2026-05-11** — recipients of HHS federal financial assistance with **15 or more employees**

## Background: why HHS Section 504 is its own law

### Where it sits in the U.S. accessibility landscape

Before this release, the U.S. array in `national-laws.json` contained three entries:

| ID | Law | Who is covered |
|----|-----|----------------|
| `us-508` | Section 508 (Rehabilitation Act) | Federal agencies |
| `us-ada-title-ii` | ADA Title II | State and local governments |
| `us-ada-title-iii` | ADA Title III | Private places of public accommodation |

None of those entries covered one of the largest categories of organizations with a web accessibility mandate in the U.S.: **private organizations that receive federal money from HHS**. This is a gap that directly affects:

- Hospitals and physician practices (Medicare Part B reimbursements count as federal financial assistance)
- Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)
- Long-term care and skilled nursing facilities
- Health plans receiving HHS funding
- Academic medical centers and research institutions with NIH/CDC grants
- Addiction treatment programs funded under SAMHSA grants
- County and regional public health programs funded with HHS pass-through money

These organizations do not fall under Section 508 (they are not federal agencies), are typically not subject to ADA Title II (they are not state/local governments), and their primary digital accessibility mandate comes from the Rehabilitation Act — specifically **Section 504** — not from the ADA.

### The statutory chain

```
29 U.S.C. § 794 (Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973)
  └─ Prohibits disability discrimination by any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance
       └─ HHS administers 100+ assistance programs (Medicare, Medicaid, NIH grants, CDC grants, SAMHSA, HRSA…)
            └─ HHS Final Rule (45 C.F.R. Part 84, 89 FR 40066, published 2024-05-09)
                 └─ 45 C.F.R. § 84.85 → web content and mobile apps must meet WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA
                 └─ 45 C.F.R. § 84.83 → self-service kiosks must be accessible
```

Section 504 has been on the books since 1973. What the 2024 HHS Final Rule added is a **specific technical benchmark** — WCAG 2.1 AA — that HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) applies when investigating complaints. Prior to the final rule, the standard was vague ("effective communication"). Now it is codified.

### Why this deadline is urgent

```
Final rule publication:    2024-05-09
Large entity (15+ emp.):   2024-05-09 + 2 years = 2026-05-11  ← IN 6 DAYS
Small entity (<15 emp.):   2025-05-09 + 2 years = 2027-05-10
```

The enforcement window is unusually tight. Unlike ADA Title II — which gave large entities two years from the 2024-04-24 publication — HHS chose a two-year window from the **2024-05-09** Federal Register date, landing on a Monday in May 2026. There has been no announced delay from HHS (a potential "Interim Final Rule" was discussed in late April 2026 per Converge Accessibility reporting, but nothing was formally published by 2026-05-05).

### How it differs from ADA Title III

ADA Title III is the law most private-sector organizations in healthcare know about for general accessibility lawsuits. But Section 504 (HHS version) is **more specific and more actionable** for HHS-funded entities:

| Dimension | ADA Title III | Section 504 (HHS) |
|-----------|---------------|--------------------|
| Covered entity | Places of public accommodation | HHS federal financial assistance recipients |
| Technical standard | No final rule — WCAG is de facto via case law | **WCAG 2.1 AA — codified in 45 C.F.R. § 84.85** |
| Compliance deadline | No deadline — exposure is immediate through litigation | **2026-05-11 (15+ employees)** |
| Primary sanction | Private lawsuits; DOJ civil penalties up to $75k–$150k | **Suspension or termination of federal funding** + private lawsuits |
| Enforcement body | DOJ | **HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR)** |

For a hospital whose revenue is predominantly Medicare and Medicaid, the prospect of losing federal funding (triggered by HHS OCR, not a private plaintiff) is the more existential risk.

## What we built — @holmdigital/standards 2.5.0

Additive changes only. No breaking changes to existing code.

### 1. New `LegalFramework` member: `'REHAB'`

**File:** [`packages/standards/src/types.ts:12`](../../packages/standards/src/types.ts#L12)

```typescript
// Before
export type LegalFramework = 'WAD' | 'EAA' | 'DDA' | 'ADA';

// After
export type LegalFramework = 'WAD' | 'EAA' | 'DDA' | 'ADA' | 'REHAB';
```

The `euFramework` field on `NationalLaw` is a required typed union. Section 504 does not map to any EU directive — it is derived from the **Rehabilitation Act of 1973**, the same U.S. statute as Section 508 (which uses `'WAD'` as a pragmatic analog to the EU Web Accessibility Directive). We considered reusing `'WAD'` for Section 504, but that would have made it impossible to distinguish Section 508 (federal agencies, WAD analog) from Section 504 (HHS-funded private sector, Rehabilitation Act). `'REHAB'` names the source statute directly.

### 2. New entry in `national-laws.json`

**File:** [`packages/standards/data/legal/national-laws.json`](../../packages/standards/data/legal/national-laws.json) — fourth U.S. element, after `us-ada-title-iii`

```json
{
    "id": "us-hhs-section-504",
    "law": "Section 504 (HHS Final Rule)",
    "fullName": "Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 — Nondiscrimination on the Basis of Disability in Programs or Activities Receiving Federal Financial Assistance (HHS Digital Accessibility Final Rule, 45 C.F.R. Part 84, 89 FR 40066)",
    "euFramework": "REHAB",
    "scope": "private",
    "lawUrl": "https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/09/2024-09237/...",
    "enforcement": {
        "authority": "us-hhs-ocr",
        "authorityName": "HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR)",
        "responsibility": "Enforces Section 504 obligations for recipients of HHS federal financial assistance…",
        "website": "https://www.hhs.gov/ocr/index.html"
    },
    "sanctions": {
        "type": "Funding Suspension / Termination / Private Lawsuit",
        "description": "HHS OCR may suspend or terminate federal financial assistance…",
        "minAmount": 0,
        "maxAmount": 0,
        "currency": "USD",
        "example": "Loss of Medicare/Medicaid funding is the primary financial risk…"
    },
    "inForce": false,
    "effectiveDate": "2026-05-11",
    "note": "Applies to private organizations — including hospitals, physician practices, FQHCs, long-term care facilities, health plans, and research institutions — that receive federal financial assistance from HHS…",
    "complianceDeadlines": {
        "largeEntity": {
            "employeeThreshold": 15,
            "deadline": "2026-05-11",
            "description": "Recipients of HHS federal financial assistance with 15 or more employees…"
        },
        "smallEntity": {
            "employeeThreshold": 14,
            "deadline": "2027-05-10",
            "description": "Recipients of HHS federal financial assistance with fewer than 15 employees…"
        }
    }
}
```

**Key design decisions:**

- **`inForce: false` at release time.** The WCAG enforcement deadline (2026-05-11) had not yet passed when the release was cut (2026-05-05). The `effectiveDate` field carries the date when `inForce` should conceptually flip. Downstream consumers doing time-sensitive compliance checks should use `complianceDeadlines.largeEntity.deadline` rather than `inForce` for precise deadline logic.

- **`scope: "private"`.** HHS-funded organizations are private entities. This aligns with the existing scope vocabulary and keeps the lookup patterns consistent: `getNationalLaws('US').filter(l => l.scope === 'private')` now returns both ADA Title III and Section 504 (HHS) — which is the correct set for a private healthcare organization to check.

- **`minAmount: 0, maxAmount: 0`.** Section 504 does not specify fixed dollar penalties. The primary sanction is funding withdrawal. Private lawsuits exist but damages vary by circuit and case — no hard cap is codified at the statute level. We follow the same zero-amount pattern as `us-508` and `us-ada-title-ii`.

- **`complianceDeadlines.largeEntity.employeeThreshold: 15`.** HHS chose employee count as the size threshold, which differs from ADA Title II (which used served population). We reused the existing `complianceDeadlines` structure but replaced `populationThreshold` with `employeeThreshold`. TypeScript's structural typing permits this — the interface uses optional named fields, not a discriminated union.

## API usage

```typescript
import { getNationalLaws } from '@holmdigital/standards';

// Get the HHS Section 504 entry
const section504 = getNationalLaws('US').find(l => l.id === 'us-hhs-section-504');

console.log(section504?.law);
// "Section 504 (HHS Final Rule)"

console.log(section504?.enforcement.authorityName);
// "HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR)"

// Check deadline for a 20-person hospital
console.log(section504?.complianceDeadlines?.largeEntity?.deadline);
// "2026-05-11"

// Check deadline for a small clinic with 8 staff
console.log(section504?.complianceDeadlines?.smallEntity?.deadline);
// "2027-05-10"

// Get all private-sector US laws (returns Title III + Section 504 HHS)
const privateUSLaws = getNationalLaws('US').filter(l => l.scope === 'private');
console.log(privateUSLaws.map(l => l.id));
// ['us-ada-title-iii', 'us-hhs-section-504']
```

## What is NOT changing

**`@holmdigital/engine` is unchanged.** The engine's `statement-generator.ts` routes U.S. laws via `euFramework === 'ADA'`. The new `REHAB` entry does not interfere with that logic — no existing output is affected.

Adding Section 504 (HHS) to engine-generated accessibility statements (for healthcare customers using `--country US --sector private`) is planned as a follow-up. The data is now available in the standards package; the engine integration comes next.

## Five content exemptions

The HHS Final Rule (45 C.F.R. § 84.88) includes five content classes exempt from the WCAG requirement:

1. **Archived content** — inactive, preserved only for reference, not updated after the compliance date
2. **Pre-existing conventional documents** — PDFs and similar files that existed before the deadline and are not updated
3. **Individualized password-protected documents** — documents created for specific individuals (e.g. custom care plans delivered securely)
4. **Pre-existing social media posts** — posts published before the compliance deadline
5. **Third-party content** — content posted by parties not under the organization's control (subject to specific conditions)

These exemptions are not captured in the current data entry but are documented in the `note` field and relevant for organizations building compliance inventories.

## Known limitations and future work

### 1. Engine does not yet surface Section 504 (HHS) in U.S. statements

`statement-generator.ts` routes U.S. private-sector customers to ADA Title III via `euFramework === 'ADA' && scope === 'private'`. A healthcare organization that is both a Title III "public accommodation" and an HHS Section 504-covered entity will not see Section 504 in their rendered statement. This is P1 work.

### 2. `inForce` is a boolean, not a date

The `inForce: false` state means consumers cannot derive "will this be in force on date X?" directly from the field. Downstream consumers doing deadline-aware checks should use `complianceDeadlines.largeEntity.deadline` and compare against the current date. A `inForceFrom` timestamp field is on the backlog.

### 3. Medicare Part B ambiguity

It is not always self-evident whether a specific payment type from HHS constitutes "federal financial assistance" under Section 504. Medicare Part B reimbursements to physician practices have been held to qualify, but edge cases exist across HHS's 100+ programs. The `note` field flags this. Organizations in doubt should consult a U.S. healthcare compliance attorney.

### 4. Verify deadline if HHS publishes an interim rule

As of 2026-05-05, HHS had not announced any delay to the 2026-05-11 deadline, despite informal reporting of a possible "Interim Final Rule" delay. If HHS publishes a delay, `effectiveDate` and `complianceDeadlines` in the JSON will need updating. Check [hhs.gov](https://www.hhs.gov/ocr/index.html) and the Federal Register before publishing any customer-facing compliance material.
